LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight Gets Its First Big Sale Since Launch

Published: July 30, 2026 · 7 min read · Deals

More than two months after its May 22 launch, LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight has finally seen a real price cut. As first reported by ComicBook.com on July 29 and confirmed independently by GG.deals a day earlier, the game is now 20% off — down from $69.99 to $55.99 — on Steam, the PlayStation Store, and the Xbox Store. It's the largest discount the game has received since release, and it comes bundled with a matching cut on the Deluxe Edition.

What's Actually Discounted

The sale covers both editions of the base game across all three of its launch platforms, but the deadlines aren't identical. On Steam, the 20% discount is scheduled to run through August 10, 2026. On the PlayStation Store and Xbox Store, ComicBook.com reports the same 20% cut runs a few days longer, through August 13. GG.deals' listing, pulled directly from Steam's storefront, matches the Steam side of that math exactly: $69.99 down to $55.99 on the standard edition, with the discount tagged specifically at -20%.

This is the game's Steam page confirming the base price points ($69.99 standard, $89.99 Deluxe) and confirming that the standalone Deluxe Edition Upgrade DLC is priced at $24.99 outside of any sale. We checked the Steam store page directly while researching this piece and, at the moment we pulled it, it was still displaying full price with no visible discount banner — which may simply reflect regional pricing, caching, or timing on Valve's end rather than a contradiction of the reporting. Both ComicBook.com and GG.deals independently list the same $55.99 figure with the same August 10 Steam end date, so we're treating that as the reliable number, but if you're checking the storefront yourself and see full price, that's not unusual during a live sale window.

Worth knowing: GG.deals also flags that the game "can be found cheaper elsewhere: up to 42% off at official stores and 50% off through keyshops, depending on the region." That's a third-party marketplace claim we haven't independently verified store-by-store, so treat it as a starting point for comparison shopping rather than a guaranteed price.

Standard vs. Deluxe: Does the Upgrade Math Still Work?

Our own Deluxe Edition breakdown covers what the $24.99 upgrade normally includes: the Legacy Collection (three themed packs — Arkham Trilogy, Batman Beyond, and Party Music — each with 7 character suits, one Batmobile, and five Batcave props) plus a season pass slot for the Mayhem Collection DLC landing September 18. At full price, buying the Standard Edition and upgrading later costs $69.99 + $24.99 = $94.98. Worth noting: our own July 27 coverage of an earlier Epic Games Store-specific sale found the Deluxe Upgrade DLC itself discounted 20% to $19.99 at that time, while Steam ran no equivalent discount that same day. If that same 20% cut is still active on the upgrade now, Standard-plus-upgrade would run $55.99 + $19.99 = $75.98; if the upgrade has since returned to full price, it's $55.99 + $24.99 = $80.98. Either way, the Deluxe Edition's flat $71.99 sale price comes out even with or cheaper than buying separately — by $4 to $9 depending on the upgrade's current price — as long as you're confident you want the full package including September's Joker and Harley Quinn content.

Is a Two-Month-Old 20% Discount a Good Deal?

Context matters here, and it's the kind our release and platform guide already tracks: the game holds an 8/10 from IGN, a "Mighty" 85 rating on OpenCritic with 97% critic recommendation, and an Overwhelmingly Positive Steam rating built on 12,665 reviews (95.8% positive) as of our last full review-count check. That reception, combined with this being the first meaningful price movement since launch, is likely why ComicBook.com frames this as notable despite the relatively modest 20% figure — big AAA-adjacent releases with reviews this strong don't always see even a 20% cut this early.

Should You Buy Now or Wait for Mayhem Collection?

If you're on the fence purely because of the September 18 Mayhem Collection DLC, this sale doesn't really change that calculus — the DLC is a separate purchase for Standard Edition owners regardless of when you bought the base game, and it's already included for anyone who owns Deluxe. What this sale does change is the cost of getting into the base game and its Legacy Collection cosmetics before that DLC lands. ComicBook.com's own read is that "the best discounts...likely won't happen until closer to the holiday season," meaning if you're comparison-shopping against a deeper future cut, patience could still pay off — but if you've simply been waiting for any discount at all, this is the first one on record.

Either way, check the deadline for your platform before deciding: Steam shoppers have less runway than PS5 and Xbox owners this time around.
