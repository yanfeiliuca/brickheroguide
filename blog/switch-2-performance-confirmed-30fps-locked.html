LEGO Batman on Switch 2: 30 FPS Confirmed, No Performance Mode Toggle

📅 September 19, 2026  |  ⏱ 6 min read

Yesterday's launch-day recap on this site flagged one thing nobody could confirm yet: how LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight actually performs on its brand-new Nintendo Switch 2 version. Less than 24 hours later, the answer is in, and it's a simple one — simpler than some players were hoping for.

The short version: Switch 2 runs a single, locked 30 FPS mode. There is no Performance Mode, no Fidelity Mode, and no way to trade resolution for frame rate like you can on Xbox Series X or PS5.

The Verdict: One Mode, 30 FPS, No Toggle

According to a report published by My Nintendo News on September 18 — the same day the Switch 2 version went live — LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight "doesn't feature a Performance Mode or a Fidelity Mode and is simply locked in at 30fps." That's a direct contrast to how the game runs on the more powerful current-gen consoles, and it left some early Switch 2 owners disappointed that the option to prioritize smoothness over visual fidelity simply isn't there.

TT Games and Warner Bros. Games have not published an official technical breakdown covering exact resolution figures for either handheld or docked play on Switch 2. Until a dedicated technical analysis (the kind Digital Foundry or similar outlets typically publish in the days after a launch) surfaces, treat any specific resolution number you see elsewhere as unconfirmed. What is confirmed, directly from day-one reporting, is the frame rate ceiling: 30 FPS, one mode, no toggle.

To put the Switch 2 result in context, here's how it stacks up against what this site has already confirmed for every other version of the game, based on our existing platform performance breakdown:

The closest parallel isn't PS5 or Series X — it's Xbox Series S, which also ships with just one 30 FPS mode rather than a dual-mode toggle, according to a Pure Xbox comparison published around the base game's May launch. Series S renders natively at 720p with upscaling to 1080p output on that hardware, a compromise attributed to the strain Unreal Engine 5's Nanite and Lumen systems put on more limited GPU memory and compute budgets. It's a reasonable bet Switch 2 is making a similar trade-off, though again, nobody has published the exact resolution numbers yet.

Why This Probably Isn't Surprising

Reaction in the comments under the My Nintendo News report was split. Some players called it disappointing, especially given the game's four-month gap between its May PS5/Xbox/PC launch and today's Switch 2 release — time some assumed might go toward a more ambitious port. Others pointed out that LEGO games have shipped at 30 FPS on Nintendo hardware for years, and that a beat-'em-up built around LEGO-style combat and puzzle-solving doesn't lean on frame-perfect reaction windows the way a competitive shooter would. One commenter also noted that the Xbox Series S — the weakest of the other consoles the game runs on — is locked to 30 FPS too, which undercuts the idea that Switch 2 is being singled out.

For context on how demanding this particular game is: it's built on Unreal Engine 5, and its open-world rendering of Gotham City has already proven to be the limiting factor on other lower-spec hardware. Our own Steam Deck settings guide found frame rates dipping below 30 in the busiest parts of the open world even on Valve's Verified-badge hardware. A single stable 30 FPS mode on Switch 2, rather than an unstable attempt at 60, may simply be the more honest engineering choice for a handheld with less horsepower than Series S.

Bonus: Batman Day Pop-Up in NYC Today

If you're near Manhattan, Warner Bros. Games is marking Batman Day 2026 with a free pop-up event today, September 19, from 12pm to 5pm EST at the Microsoft Experience Center, 667 Fifth Avenue. Visitors can go hands-on with the Mayhem Collection DLC in person and pick up free Batman-themed giveaways — timed deliberately to land the day after both the DLC and the Switch 2 version went live.

What This Means If You're Buying on Switch 2

Set expectations accordingly: you're getting the full game — base story, Mayhem Collection if you buy Deluxe, all 21 main missions, all seven playable characters — but you won't get a 60 FPS option no matter your TV or dock setup. If frame rate is your top priority and you own a PS5 or Xbox Series X, that remains the smoother way to play. If Switch 2 is your only option, or portability matters more to you than frame rate, the good news buried in today's report is that the 30 FPS this game runs at appears to be the stable, locked kind rather than an inconsistent one — the same trade-off Series S owners have already been making since May.
