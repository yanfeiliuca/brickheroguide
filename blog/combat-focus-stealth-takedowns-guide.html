Combat & Stealth Takedowns Guide: How to Master Focus in LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight

Published: August 6, 2026 · 7 min read

LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight borrows its combat almost wholesale from the Arkham series — free-flowing strikes, timed counters, and instant takedowns — but what a lot of first-time players miss is how much of that system hinges on one meter: Focus. Get comfortable filling it fast and Gotham's tougher enemy types stop being a problem. Ignore it and you'll spend every encounter getting interrupted by shielded thugs and sword-wielding mobsters. Here's how the system actually works, based on guidance from Wccftech and GAMES.GG.

The Freeflow Combat Basics

The core control scheme should feel immediately familiar to anyone who's played an Arkham game: Attack chains into combos, Counter fires off when an on-screen warning appears above an enemy's head, and Dodge gets you out of the way of anything that can't be countered, like gunfire. Gadgets have their own dedicated button and add a layer of crowd control on top of the basic punch-counter-dodge loop, per Wccftech's combat breakdown. One control quirk worth internalizing early: holding a direction while pressing an action button targets the nearest enemy in that direction, which is the difference between cleanly managing a group of six thugs and getting boxed in.

What Focus Actually Does

Every hit you land builds a circular meter around your character icon called Focus. Once it's full, pressing the Takedown button ends the fight for whoever you aim it at — regular thugs die in one hit, and bigger enemies take a massive chunk of damage. That makes Focus less of a bonus and more of the actual solution to the game's harder enemy types, according to Wccftech:

  • Shielded Enemies block every frontal attack. You have to dodge around behind them to land a hit — or skip the dance entirely with a Takedown.
  • Armed Enemies shoot from range and will break your combo the moment you get too focused on someone else.
  • Sword-Wielders throw out fast, rotating cut attacks that can't be countered at all, only dodged.

Having a Takedown charged and ready means you can remove one of these problem enemies from the fight the instant they show up, instead of trying to out-maneuver them mid-brawl. On Dark Knight difficulty specifically, where lives are limited, that difference matters a lot more than it sounds.

The Stun-to-Flurry Loop: Fastest Way to Fill Focus

Wccftech's guide lays out a specific technique for building Focus faster than standard punches allow, using Batman as the example:

  1. Fire three consecutive Batarangs at a target to stun them (fewer are needed once you unlock the Carbon Fiber Tips WayneTech upgrade).
  2. With the enemy dazed, mash the attack button repeatedly.
  3. This triggers a rapid flurry of strikes that fills the Focus meter significantly faster than standard combos — just stay ready to Counter if another enemy tries to interrupt you.

Get this Stun-to-Flurry loop down and you'll typically have a Takedown charged and waiting by the time a Shield or Sword enemy actually shows up in a fight, rather than scrambling to build Focus after the fact.

Stealth Takedowns: The Other Half of the System

Combat isn't the only way through most encounters. According to GAMES.GG's stealth breakdown, the game's level design builds in dedicated patrol routes, elevated perches, and hidden ledges specifically to reward players who avoid detection entirely. There are three distinct stealth takedown types, each tied to your position relative to the target:

Takedown TypePosition Required
Ground TakedownApproach an unaware enemy from behind
Perch TakedownDrop from an elevated gargoyle, rafter, or rooftop edge above the target
Ledge TakedownGrab a ledge below the target and pull them down

The rule for triggering any of them is the same: an unaware enemy highlights red with a prompt above their head, and pressing the Focus button (R1 on PlayStation, RB on Xbox) at that moment plays the takedown instantly and silently — no filled combo meter or Takedown gauge required. If a guard only partially notices you, a question mark appears instead; back off and wait for it to clear before trying again. Once an enemy fully spots you (question mark becomes an exclamation point), stealth shuts off for that encounter and you're back to standard combat.

Why bother with stealth: Per GAMES.GG, stealth eliminations grant larger stud rewards than standard combat kills, and a Combat skill tree upgrade generates Focus every time you take an enemy down quietly — which means a stealthy opening to a fight can hand you a charged Takedown before the shooting even starts.

The Trophy Tied to All of This

Stealth takedowns aren't just a stud farming trick — they're also tracked toward the "I am the shadows" trophy, which requires 100 stealth takedowns across the campaign, per GAMES.GG. If you missed opportunities on a first playthrough, replaying earlier missions in Free Play is the reliable way to farm the count back up. That trophy is one of 52 on PS5 and 51 on Xbox in the base game, alongside the achievements added later by the Mayhem Collection DLC.

Putting It Together

Neither system exists in isolation. A stealthy opening thins out a room and can hand you a Focus head start before combat even breaks out; a Stun-to-Flurry loop mid-fight keeps that Focus topped up so a Shielded or Sword enemy never gets to dictate the pace. Late-game encounters throw larger groups of tougher enemy types at you, which is exactly where combining both systems — rather than leaning on one alone — starts to matter most.
