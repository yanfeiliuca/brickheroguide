Mayhem Collection Designer Interview: Joker-Mite's Origin, the New Crew System, and How Absolute Mode Really Works

📅 August 30, 2026  |  ⏱ 6 min read

Our August 27 breakdown covered the headline details from Warner Bros. Games' official Mayhem Collection press release: Joker and Harley Quinn's weapons, the Sinister Pack's comic-accurate suit list, and the free Absolute difficulty. What that press release didn't include were the words of the actual developers. Brick Fanatics ran two separate pieces this week built around the same IGN Gamescom Studio interview with TT Games level designer Tim Spencer and advanced designer Chris Payne — one published August 26 alongside the trailer, a second on August 27 zeroing in on a blink-and-you'll-miss-it cameo in the footage. Together they answer several mechanical questions the press release left open.

What's new here versus our earlier coverage: the August 27 trailer breakdown and the Mayhem Collection guide already cover the confirmed weapons, the Sinister Pack, and that Absolute difficulty exists. Everything below comes from the designers' own words to IGN, as reported by Brick Fanatics, and wasn't in the official press release at all.

Joker-Mite Is a Twisted Mirror of Bat-Mite

Tucked into a few seconds of trailer footage was the first look at Joker-Mite, a character Brick Fanatics confirmed is a fifth-dimensional imp who first appeared in Batman: The Brave and the Bold, created by accident when The Joker briefly gained Bat-Mite's reality-warping powers. In the Mayhem Collection, Joker-Mite runs a chain of shops scattered across Gotham City, selling Batcave customization items, minifigure costumes, trophies, and red bricks — the villain equivalent of the base game's Bat-Mite store, but paid for with stolen cash instead of studs. The shop itself is a converted ice cream truck done up in green and purple, with Joker-Mite floating at its center surrounded by unlockables. Brick Fanatics also spotted what look like red bricks with villainous power-up effects visible on-screen, though TT Games hasn't detailed exactly what those do yet.

The Crew System Replaces Takedowns

One structural change Spencer and Payne confirmed to IGN: the base game's takedown moves are gone for Joker and Harley. In their place is a new Crew System, where players recruit fellow Arkham inmates to build out a criminal operation that does their bidding. This gives a concrete mechanical name to something the July achievement leak only hinted at — the "Welcome to the Crew" and "Full House" achievements (recruiting henchpeople and keeping ten summoned at once) that this site's achievements leak article covered without knowing what system they belonged to. Now it's clear: recruiting a crew isn't a side activity, it's the replacement for how the villains fight.

Harley's Hyenas Do More Than Fight

Bud and Lou, Harley's pet hyenas, were already confirmed as combat summons in the official press release. The designer interview adds that they're also a traversal and utility tool: Spencer and Payne described using the hyenas to unlock otherwise inaccessible areas and to open up shops for looting, beyond just fighting alongside Harley in combat encounters.

Task Force X Is Randomized Every Run

The official press release named six Task Force X members who show up during Mayhem Runs: Deathstroke, Killer Croc, Captain Boomerang, Deadshot, King Shark, and Polka-Dot Man. What it didn't say is how many actually appear in any given run, or where that list of six fits into the roster. Spencer filled in the gap for IGN: "A random selection of four [Suicide Squad members] out of 12 will arrive to try and stop you and they will hunt you down in the city. Their configuration, their abilities, the things that you're facing changes each and every time." That confirms a pool of 12 total villains, with only four hunting the player per run, and no two runs facing the same combination — a genuine roguelite-style variable rather than a fixed enemy list.

Absolute Mode Is Harsher Than "More Precision" Suggested

WB Games' official description of Absolute difficulty was vague: it's tougher than Caped Crusader and Dark Knight, and demands "greater skill, awareness, and precision in combat." The designer interview is more specific. According to Brick Fanatics' report, Absolute mode gives players a single life and removes counter and dodge prompts entirely — meaning the on-screen timing cues players rely on in the base game's combat won't appear at all on the hardest setting. That's a meaningfully different pitch than "harder combat": it's a one-life, no-safety-net mode built for players who already know Gotham's enemy patterns by heart.

Bottom line: the press release told us what's arriving on September 18. This interview tells us how it actually plays — a villain-side Crew System instead of takedowns, a 12-villain randomized Task Force X pool, utility hyenas, Joker-Mite's Bat-Mite-inverted backstory, and an Absolute difficulty that strips out combat assists rather than just raising damage. Nineteen days remain until launch.
