Detective Mode Explained: How the Sonar Pulse Actually Works in LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight

Published: August 4, 2026 · 6 min read

Every LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight review mentions it in passing, but almost none of them explain exactly how it works: the sonar-pulse scan that TT Games built to stand in for the Batman: Arkham series' iconic Detective Vision. It's the single most-used tool in the game — you'll trigger it constantly while exploring Gotham, hunting WayneTech caches, or setting up a stealth takedown — yet the game itself never fully spells out what it reveals or how to make it better. Here's the complete breakdown, sourced from a dedicated mechanics writeup and cross-checked against multiple reviews.

How to Activate Detective Mode

Detective Mode is triggered with a single button: click the right analog stick (R3) on console, or the middle mouse button on PC, according to a mechanics breakdown from Power Up Gaming. Doing so fires off a sonar pulse that scans the immediate area around your active character — not just Batman, but whichever hero you're currently controlling. The effect is temporary; the pulse highlights everything of interest for a few seconds, then fades. There's no toggleable "vision mode" you can leave switched on indefinitely — you're meant to tap it repeatedly as you move through a level or open-world zone, roughly every 10 to 20 seconds according to the same source.

What the Sonar Pulse Actually Reveals

Because the pulse only lasts a few seconds, knowing what it's capable of showing you matters more than knowing when to use it. Per Power Up Gaming's breakdown, a single activation can surface five distinct categories of information at once:

CategoryWhat It Shows
InteractablesMission goals, LEGO build spots, breakable walls, grapple points, puzzle elements
EnemiesGuard positions (even through walls or floors), awareness cones, patrol routes, and status
Collectibles & SecretsWayneTech caches, minikits, red bricks, studs, character tokens, hidden areas
Environmental CluesWeak points, explosive or destructible objects, investigation points
TraversalGrapple points, glide landing spots, vantage points

That fifth category is easy to overlook, but it's arguably the most useful for open-world navigation across Gotham's four islands — Detective Mode doubles as a quick way to spot the next grapple point or rooftop landing zone without having to scan the skyline manually.

Pairing Detective Mode With Gadgets

Detective Mode isn't just a passive scan — it's meant to be chained directly into your gadget kit. According to Power Up Gaming, once the pulse has revealed enemy positions or environmental weak points, you can immediately follow up with a Batarang to stun an enemy from stealth, use the Batclaw to pull an enemy or object toward you, or mark an explosive-gel weak wall for detonation. The scan effectively does the reconnaissance so your gadget use becomes deliberate instead of reactive, which is also why it feeds so directly into the stealth system: spotting a guard's awareness cone before you're seen is what makes take-down chains possible in the first place.

Upgrading Detective Mode at the Batcave

Detective Mode itself can be improved over time. Power Up Gaming's breakdown notes that Batcave upgrades can extend the sonar pulse's range, duration, and level of detail — meaning a fully upgraded scan will cover more ground, stay active longer, and surface more granular information than the base version you start the game with. Upgrades are purchased at the Batcave workbench using a mix of WayneTech chips, gold Skill Bricks, and studs, the same currency system that powers every character's broader skill tree. If you're grinding WayneTech caches specifically to unlock upgrades, our WayneTech Upgrades guide breaks down the chip milestones in full.

Habit to build: Because the pulse is temporary rather than a toggle, the biggest efficiency gain isn't finding a "better" way to use Detective Mode — it's simply remembering to tap it often. Firing it off every time you enter a new room, land from a glide, or clear a group of enemies costs nothing and consistently surfaces collectibles you'd otherwise walk past.

How It Compares to Arkham's Detective Vision

The comparison to Rocksteady's Batman: Arkham series is unavoidable, and reviewers have been split on how well TT Games' version holds up. TechRadar's review specifically calls out the mechanic by name, describing stealth encounters that are "paired with the Detective Mode-like area scan that will help you locate intractable items and enemies" — and flags that, combined with the game's generous stealth options, it can "make things a little too easy in places." That's a meaningfully different design philosophy from the Arkham games, where Detective Vision was a toggleable X-ray-style mode you could leave active for extended stretches. Legacy of the Dark Knight's pulse-based approach is more deliberate by design — you have to keep re-triggering it — but reviewers' consensus is that it still tips the balance toward accessibility over challenge, which tracks with this being a LEGO game built for a broad, family-friendly audience rather than a direct Arkham successor.

Worth remembering: Detective Mode scans reveal enemies "even through walls or floors" per Power Up Gaming's breakdown — TechRadar's review separately notes encountering enemies that seemed to see the player through walls on higher difficulties, which the outlet flagged as a likely bug rather than an intended mechanic. The two are unrelated systems, but worth keeping straight if you're troubleshooting unexpected enemy behavior on Dark Knight difficulty.

Whether you see the sonar-pulse approach as a smart simplification or a missed opportunity to fully commit to an Arkham-style detective layer probably depends on how much of a completionist you are. Either way, it's the tool you'll be reaching for constantly across all six chapters and the open world in between — so knowing exactly what it can (and can't) show you is worth the five minutes it takes to read this.
