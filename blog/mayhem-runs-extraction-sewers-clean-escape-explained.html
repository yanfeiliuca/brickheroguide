Mayhem Runs Explained: Extraction Sewers, Clean Escape Bonuses, and What It Costs to Gear Up

Published: September 21, 2026 · 6 min read

Since the Mayhem Collection launched on September 18, this site's own DLC guide has described Mayhem Runs in fairly broad terms: smash things as Joker or Harley, then race back to the hideout before Batman and Robin catch up. Three days in, hands-on breakdowns from outlets that have actually played the DLC through to its later systems are filling in numbers this site hasn't reported yet — specifically, how a run actually ends, what it costs to gear up permanently, and when the Suicide Squad gets involved. Here's what's now confirmed.

Extraction Sewers Decide Whether You Keep Your Studs

Every Mayhem Run launches from a planning table inside Joker and Harley's Funhouse hideout on Amusement Mile and drops you into a locked-down slice of Gotham on a countdown timer. According to a detailed post-launch breakdown by All Things How, how the run ends determines how much of what you looted you actually keep. Reach a manhole marked by a green icon and a column of green smoke — an Extraction Sewer — before the timer hits zero, and you get a Clean Escape with a +10% payout bonus. Let the timer run out and Batman and Robin drag you back in a cutscene, applying a Captured penalty that cuts your payout in half.

The outlet's own example run results make the gap concrete: a scripted, unavoidable capture on the tutorial run (no extraction points are available on it by design) banked 5,312 studs, while a second run that ended in a clean escape with seven seconds left on the clock banked 41,271 studs. Amusement Mile and East End both have working sewer exits marked on the district map, separate from the crime-objective icons.

Practical tip: check the map for your district's green sewer marker as soon as a run starts, and build your objective route so it naturally ends near that exit rather than on the opposite side of the block. All Things How also notes there's an achievement tied to using every extraction point at least once, so it's worth rotating which sewer you use rather than always heading for the same one.

What Actually Fills the Timer

The district map filters targets into categories: A.R.G.U.S. Roadblocks (armored enforcers, high stud value), Pop-Up Shops, Store Heists and Safes, Graffiti, ATMs, and Landmarks. Street clutter like newspaper stands and cash registers pays out too, but it's filler between the objectives that actually add meaningful time. Summoning recruited crew members to clear waves of thugs adds seconds directly, and explosive barrels near the docks clear groups fast when the clock is tight.

Gearing Up: The Funhouse Has Two Separate Shops

Joker-Mite — the shopkeeper imp already detailed on this site's DLC guide from pre-release Gamescom interviews — runs two distinct storefronts once you free him from the crate he's trapped in near the hideout floor. His pop-up stands out in Gotham sell single-run items and cosmetics, like the Dodge Launch boost and the Updraft, The LEGO Batman Movie, and Batman Beyond suits, all of which vanish once the run ends. His hideout station, by contrast, sells permanent equipment that carries into every future run: the Vehicle Key unlocks early and cheap, while the Grapple Hook — per All Things How's breakdown — costs 500,000 studs. Neither grapple nor glide are default abilities for Joker or Harley, so that purchase is what unlocks faster traversal to an extraction point.

Inside the hideout itself, the Toy Shop is a free customization room with Build Zones for placing your own props, while the Theater is a paid expansion gated behind a door that costs 100,000 studs — a cosmetic space rather than a functional upgrade, so it's worth buying permanent equipment first. Fully expanding the hideout unlocks the "Base of Operations" trophy.

Hench Master and the Crew System

After your first run, Hench Master — the character this site's designer interview breakdown traced back to the comic Batman: The Joker War Zone #1 — sets up in the hideout and offers to train rescued Arkham inmates as henchpeople you can summon mid-run to fight thugs, break crates, and add time to the clock. All Things How reports the related achievements require three henchpeople recruited and ten crew members active at once, so the system keeps mattering well past the opening runs.

Task Force X Shows Up After Your First Clean Escape

Escaping cleanly doesn't end the story. Per All Things How, a successful first extraction prompts Amanda Waller to deploy Task Force X, introducing Deadshot, Katana, Captain Boomerang, Mongal, King Shark, Deathstroke, and Polka-Dot Man as the next complication in later runs. Separately, hands-on impressions from BricksFanz describe five Task Force X members being sent partway through a run specifically to take you down; defeating all five activates a temporary Chaos Mode that lets you move faster, glide, and smash items instantly — useful for clearing A.R.G.U.S. roadblocks fast.

Worth flagging: the Task Force X roster named in this post-launch reporting (Deadshot, Katana, Captain Boomerang, Mongal, King Shark, Deathstroke, Polka-Dot Man) overlaps with, but isn't identical to, the six-name roster TT Games' Tim Spencer confirmed at Gamescom back in August (which included Killer Croc rather than Katana or Mongal). TT Games described a pool of 12 total members with four selected at random per run, so both lists may simply reflect different draws rather than a contradiction — we'll update the DLC guide if a definitive full roster surfaces.

Bottom Line

The core loop hasn't changed from what this site reported pre-launch: wreck a locked-down slice of Gotham as Joker or Harley, then get out before Batman and Robin catch up. What's new is the math behind it — a real +10%/−50% swing between a Clean Escape and a Captured ending, specific shop prices for permanent gear, and a Task Force X escalation that kicks in only after your first successful extraction.
