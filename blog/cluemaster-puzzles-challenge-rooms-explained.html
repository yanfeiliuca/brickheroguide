Cluemaster Puzzles Explained: The Two-Part Puzzle System Most Players Miss in LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight

Published: August 7, 2026 · 6 min read

The Riddler isn't the only puzzle-obsessed villain scattered across Gotham in LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight. Cluemaster runs his own parallel side-content track, and it's easy to miss how it actually works, because it isn't one puzzle type — it's two, and they gate each other. Miss that structure and you can end up stuck wondering why half your Cluemaster puzzle boxes refuse to appear on the map. Here's how the system is built, based on TheGamer's guides to both halves of it.

Two Different Puzzle Types, Not One

Per TheGamer, the first Cluemaster puzzle most players encounter shows up during Robin's introductory mission, "The Dynamic Duo." After finishing a set of AR challenges, what initially looks like a Riddler puzzle turns out to be a Cluemaster contraption instead — identifiable by its telltale 1-2-3 signage. That first puzzle is a mechanical symbol-matching box: rotate switches, move sliders, and swap out parts using a character's gadget until three symbols on a pillar match a reference on a nearby wall.

That's the open-world Cluemaster Puzzle Box format, and it repeats across all nine of Gotham's districts with variations — wiring puzzles that need cables routed between power points, weight puzzles where you stack values to hit a target number, code-entry puzzles solved with a character's scanning gadget, and clock puzzles where you have to match hour and minute hands to a hidden pattern. Nearly every puzzle box requires a specific character and their unique tool: Robin's staff, Nightwing's cable launcher, Batgirl's Hackarang and drone, Jim Gordon's foam sprayer, Talia's Ninja Dash, or Catwoman's whip, according to TheGamer's district-by-district breakdown.

The second format is completely different. Cluemaster Challenge Rooms are dedicated indoor puzzle spaces built around a sliding-tile mechanic: a small robot sits on a grid, and you place directional tiles — North, South, East, West — into slots to route it to a checkered goal square. TheGamer's Challenge Rooms guide covers solutions for eight of these rooms across Gotham, ranging from a two-tile solve in Newtown to multi-room sequences in the Cauldron and Old Gotham West that require breaking open shelving, powering generators with Nightwing, or plugging steam vents with Jim Gordon's foam gun before the tile slots even become accessible.

The Sequencing Trap

Here's the detail that catches players out: per TheGamer, you only unlock the rest of the Cluemaster Puzzle Boxes across Gotham after completing the first Cluemaster Challenge Room. If you've activated a district tower with Batgirl and are wondering why Cluemaster icons aren't populating the map the way Riddler and WayneTech icons do, the fix usually isn't a missed tower — it's that first Challenge Room sitting uncompleted somewhere in your save. Towers still do the heavy lifting once that gate is cleared: getting Batgirl to hack each one unlocks every Cluemaster map icon in that district automatically.

The first Challenge Room itself isn't hidden behind a locked door with no resistance, either — TheGamer notes that Two-Face's goons ambush you right as Robin tries to pry the door open, so budget for a short fight before the actual puzzle-solving starts.

What You Get for Solving Them

SystemFormatTypical Reward
Cluemaster Puzzle BoxesOpen-world mechanical puzzles, character-gatedPuzzle Box collectible, progress toward district completion
Cluemaster Challenge RoomsIndoor sliding-tile robot puzzlesWayneTech Chips, a Gold Skill Brick per room, and unlockable cosmetics

Per TheGamer, clearing Challenge Rooms nets WayneTech Chips and a Gold Skill Brick along the way, and the set as a whole unlocks cosmetic rewards including a Red Queen outfit, a Batman Serial outfit, and a LEGO Batman: The Video Game outfit — a nod to the original 2008 LEGO Batman title. If you're chasing 100 Batsuits or working through the Suits & Abilities list, these are rewards you won't stumble into just by mainlining puzzle boxes.

Where the Harder Rooms Show Up

Difficulty isn't evenly spread. Newtown's Challenge Room is a two-step solve, but Old Gotham West's room is a three-stage sequence that requires Jim Gordon to plug steam vents, a mirror-matching object placement puzzle, and a final tile section — while the Cauldron South room splits into left and right halves connected by a pulley system you have to power up mid-solve. Cauldron North is the most character-dependent of the set, requiring Nightwing to charge two separate generators and pry open doors before Jim Gordon can even reach the vents that unlock its tile slots.

Practical order of operations: Hunt down and clear your first Cluemaster Challenge Room as early as you reasonably can — before you spend a session searching a district for puzzle boxes that simply haven't unlocked yet. Everything downstream, including full district collectible sweeps, depends on that first room being done.
