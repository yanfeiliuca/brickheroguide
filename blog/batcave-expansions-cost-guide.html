All 5 Batcave Expansions in LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight, Ranked by Cost

📅 August 25, 2026  |  ⏱ 5 min read

The Batcave in LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight doesn't come fully built. You start with a functional but cramped base — a vehicle bay, the Batcomputer, Bat-Mite's Shop, and a couple of outfit racks — and four large digging robots parked against the cave walls, each waiting for a Stud payment before it clears out a new room. Nowhere on this site (or, it turns out, in most other guides) has laid out exactly what those four rooms cost and what you actually get for the money. Here's the full breakdown, cross-checked against PowerPyx's trophy roadmap and TheGamer's dedicated Batcave guide.

The Batcave's Starting Layout

Before you spend a single Stud on an expansion, the base Batcave already has five functional areas: a main center area for vehicles, an upper area with the Batcomputer (where you replay story missions), a back area housing the Challenge Board, Bat-Mite's Shop, and two large outfit display areas for your suit collection. One additional expansion is mandatory and unlocks automatically as part of the main story in Chapter 3 — it's the other four that cost real Stud money and are entirely optional.

All 4 Paid Expansions and What They Cost

Expansion Cost What It Contains
Training Area 50,000 Studs Workout benches and other exercise machines
Display Area 75,000 Studs Space to showcase unlocked trophies, plus room for more
Science Area 100,000 Studs Testing machines and workbenches
Clean Slate Area 100,000 Studs An empty, fully customizable room — build whatever you want

Add it up and buying all four runs 325,000 Studs total — roughly a third of the 1,000,000-Stud threshold for the game's biggest cumulative Stud trophy. None of the four is required for story progression or for 100% completion of a mission; they're purely a Batcave customization sink for players who've already got Studs to spare.

Why Bothering With All Four Actually Matters

The cosmetic framing undersells the real payoff: every MiniKit Trophy and WayneTech Cache hidden inside the Batcave itself only becomes accessible once the relevant expansion is built. If you're chasing 100% completion, walling off the Science Area or Clean Slate Area because you'd rather spend Studs on suits means walling off whatever collectibles are sitting inside them, too. The pieces are tucked into corners — high near the ceiling, under walkways, behind machinery — so it's worth spending a Gold Skill Brick on the Collectible Detective skill once you've expanded everything; it highlights nearby collectibles automatically and turns a room-by-room search into a five-minute sweep.

Interior details, room by room

  • Training Area is functional dressing — workout gear with no gameplay hook beyond decoration.
  • Science Area adds testing machines and extra workbenches alongside the ones you already use for gadget upgrades.
  • Display Area is where your villain trophies and other unlocked showcase items get a permanent home, separate from the story-driven Trophy Room.
  • Clean Slate Area is the odd one out: it ships empty, and what goes there is entirely up to you.

The Trophy Tied to This Grind

Buying all four expansions is worth a dedicated Bronze trophy/achievement, titled “I own the place.” It's one of 52 total trophies in the game (1 Platinum, 3 Gold, 4 Silver, 44 Bronze on PS5), and per PowerPyx's roadmap, none of the game's trophies are missable — so there's no risk of locking yourself out of it by progressing the story first. If you're working toward the Platinum, this is one to knock out on the same pass where you're farming Studs for suits and Red Bricks anyway.

Order of operations: Because the four paid rooms aren't required for story missions, most players naturally unlock them during a post-story completion pass rather than mid-campaign. If Studs are tight, prioritize whichever room's collectibles you actually want — there's no gameplay penalty for leaving one expansion for last.
