LEGO Batman Legacy's Sale Still Isn't Showing on Steam's Own Page, Two Days Later

Published: July 31, 2026 · 6 min read · Deals & Data

Yesterday we reported on LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight's first major sale: 20% off on Steam, PlayStation Store, and Xbox Store, based on matching reports from ComicBook.com and GG.deals. At the time, we noted an oddity — when we pulled up Steam's own storefront page directly, it showed full price with no visible discount banner. We chalked that up to possible regional pricing or caching. Today, checking again, the same thing happened: full price, no banner, for the second day running.

What We Checked, and When

We fetched Steam's store page for the game directly today. It lists the Standard Edition at $69.99 and the Deluxe Edition at $89.99, with an "Add to Cart" button and no discount tag on either listing. The Deluxe Edition Upgrade DLC is also shown at its full $24.99. Nothing on the page we retrieved indicates an active sale.

At the same time, we re-checked GG.deals' listing for the game, which is part of a broader "WB Games' LEGO Sale" roundup they published July 28. As of today, that listing still reads: "Regular price: $69.99, Discount: -20%, Current price: $55.99," with the overall sale window shown as ending in about two weeks — consistent with the August 10 Steam end date both ComicBook.com and GG.deals reported earlier this week.

Two sources, two different prices: GG.deals' own storefront-linked data says $55.99. Steam's page, fetched directly by us today, says $69.99. We can't independently explain the gap — it could be a rendering quirk on our end, a regional pricing difference, or something else on Valve's side. What we can say is that this is the second consecutive day our direct check hasn't matched the third-party reporting.

The Most Likely Explanations

A few possibilities are worth naming plainly, without picking a winner: Steam sale pricing can vary by region and by account sign-in state, and an anonymous, non-region-specific page load doesn't always reflect what a signed-in US shopper sees at checkout. It's also possible the discount is applied at cart or checkout rather than shown on the product page banner for this particular promotion. Either way, if you're planning to buy based on the reported 20% cut, the safest move is to check the price at your own checkout screen rather than trust any single page — ours included.

Player Counts, One Week In

Separately from the pricing question, we also pulled fresh concurrent-player data from SteamCharts today. As of our check, the game showed 6,748 players online, a 24-hour peak of 17,601, and an all-time peak of 33,053 (set at launch in May). SteamCharts' own monthly average for the last 30 days sits at 14,570.93, down 3.15% from the prior month.

It's worth being honest about what this does and doesn't show. The sale (whatever its exact price ends up being at checkout) only started a few days ago, on or around July 27–28. SteamCharts' "last 30 days" average is a rolling window that's still mostly made up of pre-sale days, so a modest month-over-month dip right now doesn't tell us whether the discount is pulling new players in — that will only be visible in next month's average. Our own prior reporting on the game's platform and reception numbers already noted the game's all-time peak sits well behind genre leaders like LEGO Star Wars: The Skywalker Saga, so a 20% sale on a two-month-old title landing during a normal seasonal lull isn't surprising on its own.

What This Means If You're Buying

If you're weighing whether to buy now, the practical advice hasn't changed much from yesterday: check the actual price shown at your checkout, on your platform, in your region, before assuming either the full $69.99 or the reported $55.99 is what you'll pay. Our own Deluxe Edition breakdown still has the full contents of what the Legacy Collection and the upcoming Mayhem Collection include if you're deciding between editions. We'll keep checking both the storefront and the player-count data as the sale window continues toward its reported August 10–13 close, and we'll update this coverage if the discrepancy resolves itself one way or the other.
