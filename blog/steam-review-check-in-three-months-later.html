Steam Review Check-In: LEGO Batman Legacy Passes 13,200 Reviews, Three Months After Launch

Published: August 21, 2026 · 6 min read · Deals & Data

May 22 to August 21 is exactly three months, which makes today a natural checkpoint for this site's running series on LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight's Steam review numbers. We last pulled the storefront directly on August 10, when the game's post-sale price reverted to full and the total sat at 13,072 reviews. Checking the same page again this morning, the count has moved to 13,243 — the first time it's crossed the 13,200 mark — and there's a small, genuinely interesting wrinkle in the recent-review trend that's worth flagging.

The Numbers, As Of This Morning

Pulled directly from Steam's product page: 13,243 total reviews across all languages (12,673 positive, 570 negative), still rated Overwhelmingly Positive overall. That's a positive rate of roughly 95.7%, essentially unchanged from the 95.7% we calculated on August 10 (12,510 of 13,072). The English-language subset, which Steam breaks out separately, now stands at 6,355 reviews, also Overwhelmingly Positive, up from 6,293 eleven days ago. Metacritic remains at 84 and the achievement count is unchanged at 52, both consistent with every prior check-in this site has run.

The Recent-Score Softening Trend Just Reversed

Our August 10 write-up flagged a "gradual, mild softening" in the recent-reviews window — the rolling roughly-30-day sample that Steam displays separately from the lifetime score. That window had gone 94% (July 26) → 92% (August 1) → 90% (August 10). Today it reads 91% of 426 reviews, still rated Very Positive. That's a small uptick, not a dramatic one, but it does break the three-checkpoint downward run this site has been tracking since late July.

Worth being precise about what this table does and doesn't show. The recent-review sample keeps shrinking — 688 down to 426 over four checkpoints — which is the expected pattern for a single-player-leaning game three months past launch as the initial wave of buyers finishes writing reviews. A one-point rebound on a shrinking sample of a few hundred reviews is well within normal week-to-week noise; it's not evidence of some renewed surge in enthusiasm. The more meaningful number is the one that's barely moved at all: the lifetime score has held at Overwhelmingly Positive, in the 95-to-96% range, at every single check-in this site has run since launch.

Review Pace: Similar Clip, Slightly Slower

The raw review-count growth gives a second, independent way to read the game's momentum. From August 1 to August 10 (nine days), the total grew by about 169 reviews, or roughly 19 a day. From August 10 to today (eleven days), it grew by 171 reviews, or roughly 16 a day. That's a modest deceleration, not a cliff — consistent with a game that's settled into a steady, unspectacular post-launch rhythm rather than one that's either spiking or collapsing.

Why this matters heading into September: The Mayhem Collection DLC lands September 18, and its Gamescom 2026 reveal — trailer plus a designer interview with Tim Spence and Chris Payne — is set for August 26, five days from today. New paid DLC tends to bring a fresh wave of reviews, both from returning players and from anyone who bought the base game during the July/August sale window and is only now getting around to writing one. The next natural check-in point is right after that DLC lands.

The Bigger Picture, Three Months In

Zooming out from the day-to-day noise: a game sitting at roughly 13,200 Steam reviews, a 95%-plus lifetime positive rate, an 84 Metacritic score, and an OpenCritic "Mighty" tier rating three months after launch is, by any normal industry benchmark, a clear commercial and critical success story. The recent-review dip-then-rebound this site has tracked over the past month is the kind of small, real fluctuation that only shows up because this site has been checking the same page on a regular cadence — not a signal of anything going wrong with the game itself.
