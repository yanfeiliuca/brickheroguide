LEGO Batman Legacy Passes 12,000 Steam Reviews While Critic Scores Hold Steady

Published: July 26, 2026 · 6 min read

Sixty-five days after launch, LEGO Batman: Legacy of the Dark Knight is well past the point where a review score can be dismissed as day-one hype. We pulled the current numbers directly from Steam and OpenCritic today, and the picture is remarkably stable: the Steam user review count has climbed past 12,600, and the critic aggregate hasn't moved from where it sat back in May. That combination — a growing review pool with an unchanged verdict — is the strongest signal a game's reception is real rather than a launch-week spike.

The Steam Number: 12,665 Reviews and Still "Overwhelmingly Positive"

As of today, Steam's own review tally for Legacy of the Dark Knight sits at 12,665 reviews across all languages and purchase types — 12,139 positive against 526 negative, for a 95.8% overall positive rate. That splits into 8,072 reviews from verified Steam purchasers and 4,593 from other sources (review keys and similar). Filtering to English-only reviews, the count is 6,123, holding at 95% positive. Steam's storefront still displays the game's overall rating as "Overwhelmingly Positive," the platform's top tier.

The more interesting number is the recent-activity window: 688 reviews logged in the last 30 days, rated "Very Positive" at 94%. That's players who bought the game well after launch-week enthusiasm faded, and they're rating it almost identically to the original wave. As of our last checkpoint in late June, the Steam total stood at roughly 11,600+ reviews — so the count has grown by close to 1,000 in about a month, without the positive rate slipping.

The Critic Side: OpenCritic Still at 85, "Mighty"

On the critic side, OpenCritic's aggregate for Legacy of the Dark Knight is 85 out of 100, carrying the site's "Mighty" designation and placing the game in the top 5% of everything OpenCritic tracks. Of the 69 critic reviews counted, 97% recommend the game. Steam's own store page still displays a Metacritic badge of 84 linking through to that aggregator — the same figure we first reported when the game set the record for highest-rated LEGO game ever on Metacritic back on May 28. The one-point gap between OpenCritic (85) and Metacritic (84) is normal; the two aggregators don't pull from identical outlet pools, so small differences like this are expected rather than a red flag.

Why Holding Steady at This Point Matters

Plenty of games see their review sentiment drift once the honeymoon period ends — bugs that weren't obvious at launch pile up in later reviews, or a vocal minority of complaints starts dragging the recent-review average down even while the all-time score stays high. We covered some of that friction ourselves in our two-month sentiment breakdown from earlier this month, which flagged recurring complaints about frame rate drops, save corruption, and the lack of online co-op. None of that has been enough to move the needle on the headline numbers: the recent 30-day positive rate (94%) is within a point or two of the all-time rate (95.8%), and the critic aggregates haven't shifted a single point since May.

Reading review aggregates: OpenCritic and Metacritic calculate their averages differently and don't track identical sets of outlets, so a one-point gap between them (85 vs. 84 here) is ordinary statistical noise, not evidence that either score is wrong.

What Could Move These Numbers Next

The next real test for both sides of this ledger lands on September 18, when the Mayhem Collection DLC and the Nintendo Switch 2 version both launch. The DLC will bring a wave of fresh Steam reviews evaluating the new Joker and Harley Quinn content, and the Switch 2 release will get its own separate Metacritic and OpenCritic aggregate, tracked independently from the PS5/Xbox/PC scores covered here. Whether that new content shifts sentiment in either direction is the next thing worth checking once it ships.
